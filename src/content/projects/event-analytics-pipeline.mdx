## Context & Background

Localz had a microservices architecture where each service emitted its own events to Kafka. A service subscribed to those messages and landed them in S3 through Kinesis Firehose; we called this the "dirty" bucket. The original design used AWS Glue to transform the data into a "clean" bucket queried with Athena. Because the results powered a customer-facing dashboard, query times of 60-120 seconds were too slow.

## Implementation

The Firehose-to-S3 infrastructure was solid, so we kept it and added a redactor to prevent PII from entering the data lake.

After reviewing different vendors, we chose Snowflake as the warehouse and dbt as the transformation layer. We built a new pipeline that extracted data from the dirty bucket, transformed it with dbt and loaded it into Snowflake.

## What Went Well
Snowflake was a great choice: large, complex queries executed in seconds rather than minutes. We could then surface more sophisticated insights, including full customer journeys assembled from several services.

## What I'd Do Differently
Glue turned out to be expensive and slow for what we needed. If I were implementing the original flow again, I would assess tools outside the AWS ecosystem before defaulting to familiar services.
